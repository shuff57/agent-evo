# Voice profile: Deyci Lopez-Rangel (dlopez)

Derived from two pieces of her graduate coursework: a co-teaching issues essay (May 2023) and an equity action plan on productive group work in a mathematics classroom (December 2023). Everything below is an observation about those two documents. Nothing here is invented. Short quotations are included as evidence for each observation.

**Use it only on her own writing, or with her say-so.** A profile records the habits of the person it was built from, so applied to anyone else it preserves the wrong things (see Voice Calibration in SKILL.md). Do not use it to produce writing attributed to her, or to sign, submit or publish anything in her name. A sample she supplies in the conversation outranks this file.

**This profile outranks the style rules in SKILL.md where they conflict,** with one caveat: it also records habits that read like AI tells but come from academic coursework register, and a few that are plain slips. See "Preserve" and "Fix" below.

## Two registers

She writes in two clearly different modes. Identify which one the text is in before editing.

- **Academic-analytic (most of both documents).** Third person plural and abstract nouns: "educators," "teachers," "students," "the collaborative setting." Long, formal, citation-backed. This is the dominant register.
- **Plain personal (the Implementation Plan's first-person passages and the closing Course Reflection).** "I gave my students a survey," "I was going through the motions and rushed throughout the entire semester." Shorter sentences, direct, no stacked abstractions. When a passage is already in this mode, leave its plainness alone and do not dress it up.

## Punctuation

- **No em dashes, no en dashes,** across both documents. The §14 ban matches her habits, so apply it freely. The one range is a hyphen: "aged 14 -18," "8:15 - 9am," "3-4 students." Leave numeric ranges as she writes them.
- **Colons introduce explanations and lists.** "In order to create a successful co-teaching approach..." and "They must think about and further plan: Which student(s)...?" Keep them.
- **Semicolons are nearly absent** and one is misused in place of a colon ("with the following questions;"). Do not introduce semicolons when replacing a dash. Use a period or a comma.
- **Commas do the joining,** including long comma-chained sentences. Do not break these up into short punchy ones.
- **Parenthetical citations sit mid-sentence or at the end,** in the form (Author, year) or (Author, et al., year): "(Yeh, et al., 2017)," "(Friend, 2007)." Never remove, merge, or reformat a citation. Never add one.
- **Straight versus curly quotes and apostrophes:** the source files use Word's curly forms. Do not treat that as a tell (see the false-positives list in SKILL.md).
- **Numbered and bare-line lists appear where she is quoting or listing questions** (survey items, vision-statement questions, DeJarnette's three strategies). Keep them as lists. They are source material, not AI bullet formatting.

## Constructions to preserve, not strip

These appear in SKILL.md's AI-pattern list but are genuine habits here.

- **Parallel section headings.** The essay uses short problem headings in parallel: "Not Enough Training," "Not Enough Time," "Not Enough Teachers." Keep the parallelism. Her headings are title case; in this profile that is her habit, so §17 does not apply.
- **Rhetorical question runs.** "Which student(s) is each teacher going to focus on? Will they both be observing and helping all of them? Who needs accommodations and modifications?" This is a deliberate way of showing how much planning is involved. Keep it. Strip a question only if its answer is the very next sentence restated.
- **Triads that mean something.** "Time, personnel, and training," "teach, implement, reflect and adapt," "their own way of learning, their own interpretation of the content, and their own preferences." Keep triads whose items are three real things from the source. Still cut a triad that pads, such as "dynamic, inclusive, and beneficial."
- **Education-field vocabulary used literally.** *Collaborate*, *inclusive*, *equitable*, *heterogeneous*, *scaffolding*, *discourse*, *accommodations and modifications*, *professional development*, *co-teaching*, *facilitating*. Many of these are the field's actual terms, not promotional inflation. Do not flatten them to plainer words, and do not substitute synonyms.
- **"Hopefully" and "I am committed to..." endings.** Her essay closes on a hope ("Hopefully, through further research of these barriers, educators can gain a better understanding...") and the action plan closes on a first-person commitment. These are her actual positions, not generic send-offs. Keep them, but tighten any added upbeat filler after them (§25).
- **Transitions like "In addition," "A second consideration is that," "However," "Therefore," "Along with."** These carry the structure of the argument. Do not delete them just because they are listed as transition words. Cut one only when it stacks with two or three others in a single paragraph.
- **Source-framed sentences.** "One study found that...," "According to the California Department of Education...," "Gammie and Matson and Nordberg argue that..." These name real sources and are the opposite of §5's vague attributions. Never replace a named source with a vaguer one, and never cut one to shorten.
- **Concrete figures and specifics.** 22,143 needed teachers, a class of 38 and a class of 27, groups of 3 to 4, Monday mornings at 8:15 to 9am, a seating chart re-randomized every unit. She hoards these. Never round, drop, or invent one.

## Sentence and paragraph shape

- **Long sentences, often 30 to 50 words,** with subordinate clauses stacked inside. She does not write staccato. Do not manufacture short-sentence drama (§31). Shorten one only when it has genuinely lost its subject or its verb (see "Fix").
- **Paragraph arc in the essay:** state the barrier, cite a study, explain what that implies for a teacher or school, close on the consequence. Keep this arc.
- **Heading, then a sentence that restates the heading, then the real content** is a habit in the Resource Review ("Teacher Facilitation Strategies..." followed by a sentence about teachers wondering how to facilitate). Apply §29 here with judgment: cut the restating sentence only when it carries nothing the next sentence doesn't.
- **First person is sparing and appears in the implementation and reflection sections only.** Do not add "I" to the analytic sections.
- **Impersonal, abstract-noun-led sentences** ("The heterogeneous nature of classrooms enhances the potential for diverse perspectives") are the dominant academic shape. That is her register, not just an AI tell.

## What to fix (these are not habits)

Her coursework files contain ordinary drafting slips. They are not voice. Correct them quietly when editing:

- Missing or wrong words: "demons flexibility" (demonstrates), "commitment of the ability to teach" (commitment to), "decisions-making," "has become in-demand across the United States" (needs a noun). The Course Reflection speaks directly to the instructor ("there was support from you as a professor"); that is addressed correctly, so leave the second person in place there.
- Run-on enumerations that lose their subject: "By establishing a culture where students feel recognized plays a pivotal role..." needs a subject.
- Tense or person drifting inside one paragraph, especially "your commitment" slipping into the Evaluate/Reflection passage (a paragraph that was apparently drafted as feedback on her own plan, then merged in). Report this rather than silently rewriting it, because it may be a pasted comment and not her prose.
- Reference-list formatting is inconsistent (author initials, "&amp;" from the file extraction, missing DOIs). Do not touch the reference list unless asked.

## AI-pattern language that IS flaggable even in her voice

Some passages in the action plan lean on stock phrasing the §-list should still catch. When these cluster, trim them:

- "fostering a dynamic learning environment," "holistic approach," "strategic imperative decision," "conducive to," "harness the collective strengths," "plays a pivotal role." Cut or restate in plain terms, per §1, §3, §7. Keep the underlying claim.
- Participial tails that add no information ("promoting an inclusive culture where each student is encouraged to embrace their unique identity").
- Restatement paragraphs that repeat the point just made in three more sentences (the second half of the Introduction and the Evaluate/Reflection list).

This is the one place the profile does not protect the writing: the shipped samples contain both a distinct human voice and a layer of coursework padding, and the padding should be thinned without flattening the first.

## Register

Formal but earnest, written by a practising teacher and student of education to an instructor. When the document being edited is a syllabus, policy, or other reference text, hold the register formal and drop the first-person reflection; the profile governs rhythm and punctuation, not the injection of voice into documents that should be neutral.
